🔬 LAB 04

Interpreting Relationships in Practice

In this Lab, you’ll independently investigate the relationship between age and comorbidity count using the same synthetic dataset as Code Along 04. You’ll decide whether Pearson correlation is appropriate, combine visual and numerical evidence, and use a causal diagram to distinguish an observed association from a causal claim.

🔬 How this Lab works

The Code Along demonstrated age and baseline severity. This Lab asks a new question: how are age and comorbidity count related? You must make and justify the analytical choices rather than reproduce the earlier result.

🔗

Map

Draw a DAG before analysing.
📈

Visualise

Assess direction, shape, and unusual observations.
⚖️

Choose

Decide whether Pearson correlation is defensible.
🧠

Interpret

Separate association from causation.

🧪 Activities

  1. State the question and identify the roles and types of both variables.
  2. Draw a DAG containing age, comorbidity count, and plausible additional variables from the dataset.
  3. Create and interpret an appropriate visualisation.
  4. Assess linearity, unusual observations, and the discreteness of comorbidity count.
  5. Choose and justify a correlation method; consider a sensitivity analysis if appropriate.
  6. Report the coefficient, confidence interval, and p-value in context.
  7. Compare what the plot, coefficient, and DAG each contribute.
💡 Remember

A correlation coefficient describes association. Your DAG records causal assumptions. Neither a small p-value nor a plausible diagram proves causation, and a large sample can make weak associations statistically persuasive.
